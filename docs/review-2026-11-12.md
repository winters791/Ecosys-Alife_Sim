### 14.2 Holes in the design

**1. A body can never catch more than one cell of light, so nothing rewards size (from the code; confirmed in both seeds).** *See also 14.10.5: the canopy's winner-take-all rule inside a cell, which this fix does not touch.* Light is shared out per cell, and a body competes only in the cell its centre is in (`share_light_by_canopy`: `left = world_.light(cell)`). A LUCA with a size-1 patch already asks for its whole cell (10.4). Demand grows with skin (r²) and upkeep with mass^0.75 (r^2.25), but income stops at one cell's light whatever the radius. So every plant settles at "one cell's worth of patch": at tick 100,000 the radius is 0.91 to 1.14 for the middle 80% of bodies in both seeds (median 1.02, largest 1.39), and the median sun patch is 1.08 to 1.09. This is the cause behind three entries in section 11: "nothing rewards being big", "bodies shrink", "most cells hold one body". It is also why the canopy is an arms race of stalks (85 to 98% fronds) and not of bodies: height is the only way left to win a cell. The contact rule already works in world units (a body reaches up to 1.5 cells); light does not. *Fix:* a body catches light over the cells its outline covers, in proportion to overlap (a small fixed stencil chosen by radius at birth, so still no per-tick geometry), and competes by canopy height in each. Then a big body shades its neighbours' cells, a tree and an understorey exist, and "photo patches shade the cell beneath them" (2.6, niche construction) comes for free.

**2. The 30% a meal loses is mostly eaten again by the eater (measured, both seeds).** Since chemistry stage 2 the lost share spills into the eater's own cell as the same chemical, and the eater takes up 5% of its cell's stock a tick through its skin (`UPTAKE_RATE` × skin) against 2.5% that drifts away and 0.5% that breaks down. World-wide, 88% of everything spilled or given off is taken up again (excreted 7,150 a row, taken up 6,320, broken down 820; seed 7 the same shares). Hunters get 74% of their food from the living and 18 to 20% from loose chemicals, largely their own spill coming back. If all of it is, an eater ends up with about 85 to 90% of what it drains, not 70%, and each step up a food chain loses almost nothing. 2.8 says "an eater keeps 70%, far more than the ~10% real food webs pass up a level"; the real figure here is higher still. With transfers this close to free there is no pyramid, and a body pays nothing for getting its energy second-hand. *Options, to test on forks (14.6):* the lost share burns as heat again and only chemicals the eater cannot burn spill; or it spills but a body cannot take up what it spilled for some ticks; or `UPTAKE_RATE` falls until drift and decay win. The first is the simplest and keeps "leavings are what you can't use".

**3. A kill's body lies where the mouth cannot reach it (from the code; measured).** Contact reaches bodies up to 1.5 cells away, but the dead are eaten only from the eater's own cell (`corpse_gain_` reads `soil_.corpse[cell]`). A sessile hunter drains a neighbour in the next cell until it dies; the corpse (the 244 of its body, plus what was left in its store) falls in the victim's cell, out of reach for good. Every hunter at tick 100,000 in both seeds is sessile (finding 5), so hunters almost never eat what they kill (3 to 5% of their food is from the dead). About 70 to 75% of all corpse energy weathers away uneaten, which is a tenth of all the energy life takes in. *Fix (a simpler rule, not a new one):* the dead are bodies with no membrane. A mouth pulls on the corpse field of the cells within its reach and arc exactly as it pulls on a living body there; `corpse_demand` and the same-cell special case go. This is 2.8 E ("kills worth coming back to") without needing a brain to come back.

**4. Half the map is one dead zone, because light and warmth fall along the same axis (from the code; measured).** The sun stands over y = 0 and light fades to 0.4 at the far edge; temperature drops 20 °C along the same axis. So there is one gradient, bright-and-warm to dim-and-cold, not two. In both seeds the far quarter of the map holds no bodies at all and the third quarter 5 to 9% of them. With `--light uniform` (seed 7, 60,000 ticks) the same world holds 4,620 creatures and 115 established species against 1,570 and 30, and 29% of its bodies live in the far half. Uniform light is also about 40% more light in total, so part of that is simply more energy; but a *more* uniform field gave nearly four times the species, and cold alone did not keep life out of the far half. The stacked gradient is not making niches, it is removing ground. *Fix:* let the two fields vary independently, so bright-cold and dim-warm places exist: for example temperature by latitude and altitude as now, light by slope shade plus a pattern on another axis (east to west, or broad patches). No new rule and no cost per tick; the fields are static. One seed: confirm on six.

**5. The "hunters" of 2.8 are not the hunters in the world (measured, both seeds).** At tick 100,000 seed 42 has 83 bodies living mostly on the living and seed 7 has 25. None of them can move. The worlds hold 5 and 2 movers (at ticks 50,000 to 60,000 they held 122 and 15: the boom had passed). Predation here is a rooted body with a mouth draining the neighbour it was born beside. 2.8 is written for a mover that gorges and then wastes its kill; see 14.4 for what the probe says about that.

**6. Matter limits nothing (measured).** *See 14.10.2 for the deeper version: elements in fixed ratios.* 91 to 95% of all matter sits in the soil as A at tick 100,000. Living bodies and stores hold under a tenth. Vents more than double the world's matter and "life doesn't seem to mind" (section 11) for the same reason. Since chemistry stage 1, matter is the container energy comes in: A to D are one kind of matter at four energy levels, so the matter book mostly repeats the energy book. The things 2.1 wanted from matter (a niche for decomposers, poor ground worth fighting over, "what bodies can be built from") have no hook. Corpses also free their own matter by weathering, so no plant ever depends on a decomposer. *If matter is meant to bite:* one structural element that carries no energy, needed per unit of body and returned only by decay (Liebig's minimum), and weathering slow enough that eating the dead is what returns it. If it is not meant to bite, say so and drop "more than one nutrient" from the list. Uday's call; nothing else depends on it yet.

**7. No leavings exist, and idea 2.9 A will not make them (arithmetic; tested on two seeds).** *14.10.2 argues the ladder fix below treats a symptom; test both on forks.* 2.9 has the reason right: a partial burn is strictly worse. But the planned cure, upkeep ∝ rate × (step / 222)², has two gaps.
   - *How small the cost is:* a LUCA's enzyme costs about 4% of its upkeep (10.4). No change to a 4% cost can pay for throwing away a third or two-thirds of the energy in every unit of food.
   - *Chains:* under any cost that grows faster than the step, three short enzymes in one body (D → C, C → B, B → A) burn fully for a third of the upkeep of D → A. The body that gains is the one that carries the whole chain, and it gives off nothing.
   - *Test:* with that cost rule built into the private copy (seeds 42 and 7, 60,000 ticks), loose B is 1.6 and 0.4 units a world and loose C 0.5 and 0.4, against 0.8 / 0.3 and 0.7 / 0.1 without it. Nearly every body still carries A ↔ D (3,910 of 3,980 and 4,060 of 4,150). Nothing changed.
   - *Underneath:* enzymes are reversible at one rate, so whatever a body can make from light it can also burn. A producer is always its own consumer; a body only ever "can't burn" something it got by a partial step, and a partial step never pays.
   - *What the textbook case needs* is diminishing returns down the ladder plus a real limit on machinery. The smallest version: **uneven levels** (for example A 0, B 30, C 90, D 222, so the top step releases most) and **one rung per enzyme** (no enzyme spans more than neighbouring levels; a full burn is three enzymes). At today's flat upkeep per unit of rate, the bottom rung then earns a quarter of what the top rung earns for the same cost, so a well-fed body drops it and gives off B, and a body that lives on B has something to eat. (LUCA would then start with three enzymes, or store a poorer chemical.) Whether that is enough needs a fork test; it may also need enzymes to take room (total rate limited by body volume) so that power, not food, is what runs short. Two-input reactions stay as the way to make side products.
   - Until some rule makes leavings exist, 2.9 B, E and F and water stage 2 (2.7) have nothing to act on.

**8. Every sense is a hand-made output, so each new thing to notice needs new code (from the code).** The brain does not see; its outputs are gains on built-in steering rules ("lean up the slope of field X"), one enum entry and one `lean(...)` call per field. The file already lists the gaps this leaves: waste can't be sensed, poison can't be sensed, vents can't be sensed, water depth will need an input, mates will need a hue sense, signals need a scent sense. That is the "one gene per way of feeding" problem (2.1.1) again, on the sensing side. *The general rule, now designed in 14.9 (Uday's proposal, 2026-11-12):* a sensor part carries a **channel** (which field it reads) and feeds the brain what it reads at a point; the brain drives effectors directly (thrust, turning, the mouth, sleep). No rule steers. A vent dweller then finds its vent by sensing C, a cleaner finds leavings, a signal is a chemical nobody burns, and none of them is a new feature. (This review first suggested generic "lean" outputs, one per sensor; 14.9 replaces that, since a lean output is still steering written into the rules.) Two smaller things belong to the same change:
   - **The kin sense reads species ids** (10.4 says so). A label the game invents is acting as a law, and `SPECIES_THRESHOLD` tunes behaviour. Kin should be told by something a body can perceive: likeness of hue or of what it gives off. Then species are observation only again, which 9.1 promises.
   - **A brain needs a motor** (`has_brain` requires thrust). 2.8 C (rest on purpose) and 2.9 C (a mouth the brain can close) assume brains on bodies that, by finding 5, are all sessile. Outputs should drive whatever effectors a body has: a mouth, sleep, breeding, a motor. A rooted body with a mouth and two links is then a flytrap that can wait.

**9. Rule changes are being judged at the edge of what six fresh seeds can show (from this file).** 10.7 says it repeatedly: "the spread between seeds is as wide as the difference", "one sweep", "measured on a build that also held uncommitted work". Four chemistry stages, the designer, the vault and world events all landed in two days, each judged on one sweep, some against a baseline that had moved. Several built mechanics are, by their own numbers, used by nothing (14.3). The sweep script itself is not in the repo. The cure is the harness in 14.6: paired forks of the same mature world separate a rule's effect from the luck of the seed, and take minutes.

**Smaller, checked and found not to matter much:** a body dies in any tick its enzymes cannot release its costs, even with food in store (10.4 still says "a creature at zero dies"). That could have been an instant-death switch for poison or fouling. Measured: 0.6 to 1.6% of deaths happen with enough in store to pay, nearly all old bodies whose wear outgrew their enzyme rate; 8 and 11 deaths in 100,000 ticks had poison as the largest cost. Leave it.

### 14.4 The queued designs, re-read against the measurements

- **2.8 A (satiation) should not be built first.** 2.8's own first step was a probe, and it was run. Bodies with a store at least 95% full do 0.2% (seed 42) and 0.4% (seed 7) of all draining, and under 0.1% of any meal is spilled for want of room. A full hunter that keeps killing is not what happens, because nothing is ever full: a body breeds every 8 ticks once its store is worth `repro_threshold`, and breeding empties the store. Capping the drain at the room left would change about one meal in three hundred. The boom-and-bust of movers (10.7) is real, but its cause is not waste at the mouth. Before B or C, probe the other two causes on six seeds with the harness (what share of a mover's upkeep is standing gear; how long movers live between meals), and note that a body that could fast for a week would first have to not turn every surplus into a child, which `repro_threshold` and storage already let evolution choose. If it never chooses it, the question is what makes waiting pay.
- **2.8 C and 2.9 C** are one change: stage 2 of the sensor and motor brain (14.9), which first lets a brain exist without a motor.
- **2.9 A** as written: see 14.2.7. **2.9 D (adhesion from birth)** is independent of all this and still the cleanest route to cooperation between kin.
- **2.7 water.** Stage 1 (sea level, light under water) and stage 3 (motors by medium) stand on their own and answer finding 4's need for real barriers and refuges. Stage 2's judge ("loose B and C rise in the sea") cannot pass while nobody gives off B or C; move its expectation to after the chemistry is fixed. Every stage adds per-cell fields, so do 14.5 item 1 before any of it. Nine stages is a lot of design for a world whose first-order problems are findings 1 to 4; build 1 and 3, then look again.
- **9.4 sex** depends on water, signals or a hue sense, and stable neuron ids. Nothing in this review moves it earlier. The generic senses of 14.2.8 give it mate preference without a new sense.
- **2.6:** shade from sun patches falls out of finding 1's fix; signals fall out of 14.2.8; carrying matter has no point while matter limits nothing (finding 6).

### 14.5 Performance

Timings are from the cloud copy; ratios carry over, absolute numbers are about 1.8× better on the Mac.

1. **The tick pays per cell, whether or not anything lives there.** With about 25 creatures: 0.048 ms/tick at 128², 0.240 at 256², 0.977 at 512². The design's starting scale is 512² (6.5), and at that size an empty world costs as much as 7,000 creatures (135 ns each in a 24,700-creature world on the same machine). At 128² it is already more than half of the average tick in the 5,000-tick baseline run, so the "ns per creature" figures in 10.7 mix the two. It breaks 7.3 rule 7 (fields every 5 to 10 ticks). Where it goes, every tick: five per-cell arrays cleared (`biomass_`, `seen_` and the three demand arrays, one of them four wide), `soil_.waste` recomputed for every cell, two per-cell passes in `SpatialGrid::build`; and every 8 ticks two full passes over a 16-byte `Mix` per cell in `spread_dissolved` plus a full pass in `weather_corpses`. *Fix:* clear and rebuild only the cells that held a body last tick or hold one now (the grid already knows them); keep a list of cells with loose chemicals or corpses and walk that; recompute `waste` on the field tick, for touched cells. *Judge:* an empty 512² world under 0.1 ms/tick, and runs otherwise identical tick for tick. Do this before water, which adds fields.
2. **Camouflage is worked out for every body every tick, for almost no reader.** `shows()` and `ground_colour()` take 12.5% of all sim time in a 40,000-tick profile: 107 million calls, while `decide_movement` (the only reader, through `seen_` and the kin sense) ran 0.84 million times. A rooted body's visibility changes only when its ground changes colour. *Fix:* keep it per body and refresh it when the body moves, grows, or on the field tick; or compute it only inside the senses that read it. Same world, an eighth less time. *Superseded 2026-11-12: camouflage is to be removed outright (14.8), which takes this cost away with it.*
3. **The hot loops read 264-byte structs.** `Creatures` is arrays of fields, except `pheno` (264 bytes each), `brain` (452 bytes each, for every body, though only a few percent have a brain) and `body` (264). Phases 1a, 1c and 2b touch `pheno` for every creature, which is the "scattered memory access" 10.7 already blames. *Fix:* pull the dozen fields the tick needs (mass, upkeep, light capture, canopy, skin, absorb, capacity, burns, the enzyme table) into their own arrays or one small hot struct; keep the rest cold; give brains and body plans their own short arrays indexed from the creature, so 3,500 plants do not carry 1.6 MB of empty wiring.
4. *(Done in step 0.3: every draw is keyed, 14.10.4.)* **Phase 1 draws from the shared `Rng`,** so it cannot be split across threads without changing every run (the plan in "Next scaffolding"). `decide_movement` calls `rng_.normal()` per mover in creature order. *Fix:* draw the wander step from a hash of (creature id, tick), as a newborn's first angle already is. Phase 1 then reads only, and tiles can run in any order. Gene transfer's per-contact draw (14.3) is the same kind of thing in phase 2a. *Widened by 14.10.4: every draw, not only these, goes counter-based, in step 0.*
5. **Rooted bodies are recomputed every tick though nothing about them changes that fast.** Nearly all bodies never move (under 1% of creature-ticks in the profiled run decided a movement); their contacts are the same pairs every tick (50 million `contact_flow` calls for 107 million creature-ticks) and their light changes over a 600-tick day. Stepping brainless rooted bodies every fourth tick with four ticks' flows would cut most of the per-creature cost, more than threads will on eight cores. It touches the books and the contact shares, so it is a design change, not a refactor: after items 1 to 3, and only if 10,000 creatures at 10× is still out of reach.

Items 1, 2 and 3 are refactors that must reproduce today's runs exactly; item 4 changes the stream once; item 5 changes results. Do them in that order.

**Status, 2026-11-12 (late):** item 1 built and exact (empty 512² world 1.44 → 0.056 ms/tick, measured while the ablation held every core; details in CLAUDE.md 14.5 "As built"). Item 2 went with camouflage (14.8). Item 3 left: `Creatures` is already split by field and a profile of a full world puts the time in the per-body rules, not in reading cold phenotype fields (CLAUDE.md 14.5).

Fronds are still most bodies, so the canopy's winner-take-all (14.10.5) is still the stronger force. On fresh seeds it is cheaper than on forks (+44% a tick), because the world is not fuller.
- **The dead in reach passes:** what fed on corpses weathering now feeds scavengers and hunters. The population falls 12%: less matter goes back to soil by weathering, and more through bodies.
- **Light across fails:** the dead half moves from cold to dark instead of going away, and the dark, cold quarter is as empty as before. 14.2.4's diagnosis stands (light and warmth falling together make a dead half), but this fix makes a different dead half. Options: a gentler light gradient (`gradient_min`), or warmth from height rather than from y, so cold is in the hills, not across half the map.

### 14.10 Points adopted from an outside review (2026-11-12)

Uday brought in a second critique of the design. Much of it matches the findings above: the brain as the most prescriptive part (14.2.8, 14.9), kin reading species ids, snapshots and forks first (14.6), the 2.9 A cost curve being prescription by another route (14.2.7). These five points are new or go further, and are adopted for now. Points 6 to 8 take up its other proposals, with the catches found on checking them.

1. **Generations, not just seeds.** Measured on seeds 42 and 7: at tick 100,000 the median body is generation 31 to 32, and the furthest lineage is at 60 to 76. That is too few to conclude that something won't evolve, and it weakens this review's own two-seed results most of all. On the Mac a run costs about 0.2 to 0.4 ms/tick, so 30 seeds × 1,000,000 ticks is roughly 20 minutes to an hour on 8 cores (more if worlds keep filling: 5,760 creatures a world at tick 200,000, 10.7).
   - *Rule:* a negative result that is **empirical** (grazing, a borrowed part spreading, windfalls being sought, hunters persisting) is judged on long runs and many seeds before anything is shelved for it.
   - A negative result that is **arithmetic** needs a rule change, not a longer run: nothing gives off B or C because a partial burn is strictly worse (14.2.7), and neutral exchanges have nothing to exchange.
   - Snapshots (14.6.1) make this cheap: extend the shelf of mature worlds instead of starting again.
2. **Stoichiometry: elements needed in fixed ratios.** The deeper version of 14.2.6 and 14.2.7.
   - Chemicals A to D are one kind of matter at four energy levels, and a body is built of whatever its store holds (2.1.1). So B and C are only worse fuel than D, and "diet" can only mean which rung a body burns. Section 2.1 first promised "a few abstract nutrients"; the ladder replaced that.
   - Niches come from needing several conserved elements in fixed ratios (Liebig's minimum, ecological stoichiometry): a body is limited by the scarcest element in its cell, and what one lineage has left over is what another lacks.
   - It makes the ladder fix of 14.2.7 a treatment of a symptom. It is the bigger change: every `Mix`, store, corpse and per-cell field grows, and the books gain a column each.
   - To design before 2.9 is built, and to test on forks against the ladder fix (step 4).
3. **A thin playable loop, early.** The likeliest dead end is the project never becoming a game. The plans hold over two dozen further sim stages (water 9, sex 5, 2.8 and 2.9 eleven ideas, the brain 3), while goals and influence are unbuilt. Step 0b pulls a minimal loop in right after the harness: save and load in the viewer, one scenario, goal checks on the existing detectors.
4. **Counter-based randomness for every draw.** 14.5 item 4 fixed the wander draw only. Any re-sort of the creature arrays (the cache fix of 14.5 item 3, threading by tile) changes the order of draws from the shared `Rng` for mutation, births and gene transfer, and so changes every run.
   - Draw every random number from a hash of (seed, tick, creature id, purpose), as a newborn's first angle already is. Order then never matters, and sorting or threading keeps runs identical.
   - It changes every run once, so do it in step 0, before baselines and the shelf of mature worlds are taken. The sim's `Rng` state then drops out of snapshots; the events' stream can follow the same rule.
   - **As built (2026-11-12, `core/rng.hpp`):** `draw_key(seed, purpose, tick, a, b)` hashes its inputs with splitmix64's mixer; `key_uniform(key)` gives one number from a key, and `Rng rng(key)` a short stream when a draw needs several (a birth's mutations and landing, an event). The purposes are the `Draw` enum: `Start`, `Wander`, `Transfer`, `Birth`, `Windfall`, `Event`, `EventTrigger`. The sim and the events each keep only a 64-bit draw seed (made from the world seed, kept apart so events at rate 0 still change nothing), and that is all the snapshot keeps of randomness: snapshot version 2, and version 1 files are refused with a message, since they cannot be continued exactly. `mutate` and `transfer_part` still take an `Rng&`. Tests: `test_keyed_draws` (keys depend on every input, numbers evenly spread, neighbouring ids unrelated), `test_snapshot_round_trip` and `test_laws` (exact continuation). Headless, events at rate 2: 5,000 ticks, save, load, 3,000 more matches 8,000 straight, `powers.txt` included.
   - **What this does not yet make order-free:** new ids are handed out in the order births are found in the arrays, and per-cell sums (light demand, biomass, uptake and corpse shares) add floats in body order. A re-sort of the arrays (14.5 item 3) still changes runs through these two, unless births are numbered by parent id and the sums are made order-free (or the re-sort is accepted as one more change of baseline). Threading phase 1 by tile is now possible as far as randomness goes.
5. **Canopy, the fertility curve, and sex without it.**
   - *Winner-take-all canopy* is a one-axis arms race with a cheap stalk, and it has one winner: 85 to 98% fronds, and young that starve in shade (10.7). 14.2.1's footprint fix leaves it as it is inside a cell. To test on forks: each layer passes a share of the light on down by how much leaf it has (Beer–Lambert), so the understorey gets something and height is one advantage among several. Passing 30% down was tried once (10.7) against a different problem, starving young, and did not end the creepers; folding has since dealt with those.
   - *The fertility curve in 9.4* scripts reproductive isolation with hand-set distance weights. Build 9.4 stage 1 without `FERTILITY_LIMIT`: recombining mismatched brains, enzyme sets and parts lists may give broken children by itself (hybrid breakdown from genes that only work together). Add the curve only if nothing isolates. Such breakdown takes many generations to build up (point 1).
6. **Single-step enzymes with leaky stores,** in place of 2.9 A. An enzyme may only join neighbouring rungs (D ↔ C, C ↔ B, B ↔ A), and every store leaks a small share through the skin into its cell each tick. A full burn then needs three enzymes, and a body that loses one gives off the chemical it can no longer burn, so dependency could come from gene loss with no cost curve. LUCA would start with three enzymes. Three catches to settle first:
   - *Losing an enzyme is not cheap at today's prices.* Enzyme upkeep is about 4% of a LUCA's budget, and dropping B → A gives up a third of every unit of food. Losing it pays only where bodies are limited by enzyme rate, not by food, so enzymes need to take room (total rate capped by body volume, as 14.2.7 suggests).
   - *Plants cannot drop the bottom rung.* Light has to run A → B first, so plants keep all three; only eaters could lose one. That is where fermenters would be, so it may be enough.
   - *Leaks are eaten back.* A leak lands in the leaker's own cell, where 88% of spills are taken up again (14.2.2). Settle the re-uptake question (roadmap step 2, item 3) first.
   Untested. A fork candidate for step 4, beside uneven levels and stoichiometry.
7. **A mantle reservoir** for the matter vents bring up (Known weak spots: matter piles up around vents). Soil A seeps down into one world-wide pool, and vents bring up only what the pool holds. Matter is then conserved exactly and the pile-up is bounded. It is cleaner than water stage 4's sediment sink, which would be a second patch on top of the first. It needs three more pieces:
   - *A brake on seeping.* If A seeps down everywhere and only vents bring it back, a world with no vents loses its soil slowly and for ever. Seeping should slow as the pool fills (towards a set size), or happen only near vents.
   - *A starting stock,* or a new vent brings up nothing.
   - *Meteors still bring matter* (`land_meteor`), so the `matter_brought_up` exception stays unless their cargo is drawn from the pool too.
   Uday chose to leave the pile-up for now (2.1.1); this is the fix to take when it matters.
8. **An ablation pass,** the first use of `Laws` (step 0, item 5). Switch each rule off in turn across 30 fresh seeds and delete the ones whose absence changes nothing.
   - `MOVER_SUN` needs no test: it is already off (1). Delete it. *Deleted 2026-11-12; a snapshot that lists it still loads (an unknown law is skipped).*
   - The list: light lost while moving (`MOVING_SHADE`, `FULL_STRIDE`), folding, `bite`, windfalls, gene transfer, neutral exchanges, `repair`, `mutability`. Each needs a switch in `Laws`.
   - `bite` is not a pure copy of mouth size: it raises pull on the living only, not on the dead, and costs less per unit of pull than a bigger mouth, with no wider arc. Ablation settles whether that earns its keep.
   - *Fresh worlds, not forks:* a mature lineage adapted to a rule crashes when the rule is taken away, which looks like the rule mattering when it may not.
   - **Switches (built 2026-11-12, all laws, 1 = as built, 0 = off):** `USE_BITE`, `USE_REPAIR` and `USE_MUTABILITY` hold the gene at 0 in `clamp_genome`, so no body can carry it; `USE_NEUTRAL` stops neutral exchanges from running (`compile_phenotype`); `GENE_TRANSFER` multiplies `gene_transfer_chance`. Already switches: `MOVING_SHADE=0` (light lost while moving), `UNFOLD_TICKS=0` (folding), `--windfall 0`. At their defaults runs are identical to before, tick for tick. Checked over 20,000 ticks: each switch takes its gene or rule to exactly 0, and `USE_NEUTRAL=0` gives an identical run, because no body ran a neutral exchange.
   - **The run:** `tools/ab.py --seeds <the 30> --ticks 100000 --out ab_out/ablation` with one `--variant` per switch above (the 30 are `THIRTY` in `tools/shelf.py`: 42 / 7 / 99 / 1 / 2 / 3 and 4 to 28 less 7).

**Results (run 2026-11-12, finished the same evening; `luca/ab_out/ablation/summary.csv`).** 30 fresh seeds, 100,000 ticks, each switch against the same seed with every rule on; means over the last fifth. "t" is the mean change over its standard error (sd / √30): about 2 or more is a real effect, under 1 is noise. "Seeds" is how many of 30 moved the same way as the mean (15 is a coin toss; 21 or more is unlikely by chance). Control: 2,798 creatures, 68 species, 72 hunters, 13 scavengers, 262 mixed feeders, 21 movers. Worst audit error 2.4e-7 in every arm.
   - **Folding off (`UNFOLD_TICKS=0`): matters most.** Movers 21 → 82 (t 3.1, 24 seeds), species −17% (t 2.7, 21), hunters −25% (t 3.1, 22), population −10% (20). The drifting plants return. Keep.
   - **Gene transfer off (`GENE_TRANSFER=0`): matters, and the other way from what 14.3 expected.** Hunters 72 → 108 (+50%, t 4.6, 21 seeds), mixed feeders +44% (t 4.1), living eaten +42%; species −9% (21 seeds). With transfer on, hunters take up sun patches from the plants they eat and their young pay for them, so transfer holds hunting down. Not dead weight; whether it is a rule worth keeping is a design call.
   - **Light lost while moving off (`MOVING_SHADE=0`): small but real.** Movers 21 → 35 (t 3.2, 21 seeds), species +8% (19). Folding already does most of its work; the two overlap.
   - **`bite` off: weak.** Hunters −17% (t 1.9), mixed feeders −21% (t 2.3), species −12% (18 seeds), population unchanged. Bite is used by the mixed feeders; borderline.
   - **Windfalls off: weak.** Mixed feeders −19% (t 2.1), hunters −15% (t 1.9), loose B −38% (21 seeds); population and species within noise. Not a scavenger rule in practice: windfalls mostly feed mixed feeders.
   - **Neutral exchanges off (`USE_NEUTRAL=0`): nothing.** Every number within ±8%, none with t over 1 or more than 17 seeds. (Not exactly identical to control: somewhere in 100,000 ticks some body ran one; at 20,000 none had.) Candidate for shelving.
   - **`repair` off: nothing.** Every number within −9%, t under 1. Candidate for shelving.
   - **`mutability` off: nothing measurable.** Population +5%, movers +29%, but t under 1 everywhere and 14 to 17 seeds. Candidate for shelving, though its effect may only show over longer runs (14.10.1).
   - *Caveats:* 100,000 ticks, not 1,000,000; fresh seeds only.
   - **Deleted (2026-11-12, Uday's go-ahead):** neutral exchanges, `repair` and `mutability`, with their switches. `USE_BITE` and `GENE_TRANSFER` stay as laws. Old creature files and snapshots still load. The step-1 baseline and step-2 fork screen run that evening used the build before the deletion.
   - *The shelf, built before the ablation in the same chain* (`tools/shelf.py`): `luca/worlds/` holds `plain_s<seed>_100000.snap` for all 30 seeds, and `vents_` and `events_` worlds for seeds 42 / 7 / 99 / 1 / 2 / 3, all at 100,000 ticks. Not yet extended to 1,000,000 (`tools/shelf.py` with no `--ticks`; several hours on 8 cores).
