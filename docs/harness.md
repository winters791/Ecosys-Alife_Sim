### 14.6 Development harness: snapshots, forks and laws (design; Uday's request, 2026-11-12)

**The aim:** change one rule or one number and see what it does to a mature ecosystem in minutes, with enough precision to tell the rule's effect from the luck of the seed.

**What exists already.** `Simulation` is a plain value: every member is a vector, a string or a number, so it can be copied. Tested on the private copy: a world copied at tick 6,000 and run beside the original for 3,000 more ticks ends identical (1,635 creatures, stored energy and total matter equal to the last digit). So **an in-memory fork works today** (`Simulation fork = sim;`), and the knockout experiments of 2.9 need nothing more than that plus "remove species X". What is missing is a file, a way to change a law without rebuilding, and a tool that compares.

**1. Snapshot file (`core/snapshot.*`, `--save FILE`, `--save-every N`, `--load FILE`).** State only, never derived values, by the same principle as creature files (section 5):
- *Saved:* the config; `tick_`, `next_id_`, `next_lineage_`, `matter_brought_up_`, the arrived-between-ticks sums; the sim's draw seed (since step 0.3; the sim `Rng` state before); the events' draw seed, rate, counts and pending notes; the world's layers (height, local heat, vents, sun multiplier, climate offset); the soil (`nutrient`, `corpse`, `dissolved`, `vent_made`); for every creature its position, heading, `moved`, `ate`, `hurt`, `unfold`, `wander`, `sleep`, `body_temp`, store, tissue, age, fed counters, kills, children, id, lineage, species, generation, genome, brain state, and **how far it has grown** (today that lives only inside `Phenotype::grown`, which is derived; it must become state); the power system's pending, active and logged commands; the whole species registry, extinct species included; both chronicles, the watch list and the detectors' flags.
- *Recomputed on load:* shade, drainage, light and temperature fields; `corpse_energy` and `waste`; every phenotype, brain plan and body plan; `energy`.
- *Format:* tagged sections with a version and a length, so an old reader skips what it does not know and a new one fills defaults for what is missing. Genomes are written by the same field list as creature files, so a new gene is added in one place. A short text header: seed, tick, build, and the laws in force.
- *Two ways to load.* **Exact** (same build and laws): the run continues tick for tick; the test is run 15,000 ticks straight against run 10,000, save, load, run 5,000, and compare. **Re-ruled** (different laws or code): phenotypes are compiled under the new rules; a store that no longer fits its capacity spills the excess into its cell so both books still close; the audit restarts from the loaded state; the chronicle notes that the laws changed.
- *Size:* a few megabytes for a 128² world with 3,500 creatures and its species history.
- This is also the player's save and load (section 11, point 6) and "load the world 100 ticks before the bug".
- **As built (2026-11-12, `core/snapshot.*`):**
  - *Format:* the text header (`luca-snapshot 1`, tick, creatures, established species, build time, then one `config NAME VALUE` line per setting, ended by a blank line; `head world.snap` shows it), then binary sections `CLOK DETS EVNT WRLD SOIL CRTR POWR SPEC CHRN ALRT WTCH`, each a four-letter tag and a length. Every record (a creature, a genome, a part, a species) carries its length, and each is written and read by one field list (`io_genome` and the other templates in `snapshot.cpp`), so a field added at the end of a record reads as its default from an older file. Config is matched by name; an unknown setting is ignored. A newer version, a cut-short file or a creature of an unknown species is refused, not half-read. About 1.6 MB for a 128² world of 1,900 creatures at tick 10,000.
  - *`grown`* is written per creature as state. In memory it still lives in `Phenotype::grown`; loading compiles `compile_phenotype(genome, grown)`, which gives back the same stats because the clamp on `grown` is idempotent.
  - *Gene transfer needed one piece of new state.* A body that took up a blueprint keeps the stats it was built with, and its brain and body plans from the genome it was born with, while its genome holds the new part (10.4). To rebuild it, the sim now keeps, for each such body still alive, its birth genome and the genome its stats were last compiled from (`Simulation::borrowed_`, by creature id; set on its first transfer, updated when it grows, dropped when it dies). No rule reads it; `borrowed_count()` is for tests.
  - *Exact round trip, tested:* `test_snapshot_round_trip` (in `test_life`) runs a 64² world with events at rate 4, a vent, a ramp still running and a power still pending at the save, a followed species and bodies carrying borrowed blueprints. Every body loads with the same stats, brain and body plans; the loaded world saves to the same bytes; after 2,500 more ticks both worlds again save to the same bytes. Headless, seed 42 with events at rate 1: 15,000 ticks straight against 10,000 ticks, save, load, 5,000 more give identical `creatures.csv`, `chronicle.txt`, `species.csv` and `powers.txt`, and every metrics column except `ms_per_tick`.
  - *Re-ruled loading* recompiles every body under the loading build's rules and spills any store that no longer fits into its cell. Since step 0.2 the header also lists every law in force (`law NAME VALUE`); loading restores those laws and then applies `--set` / `--laws`, and when anything changed the chronicle says so (*"The laws of the world change: TRANSFER_EFFICIENCY 0.7 to 0.5."*). Tested in `test_laws` and headless (a world saved at 5,000 and continued under `TRANSFER_EFFICIENCY=0.5`: audit error under 1e-7).
  - *Not done:* save and load in the viewer.

**2. Laws you can set without rebuilding (`core/laws.hpp`, `--set NAME=VALUE`, `--laws FILE`).** The tunable `constexpr` numbers listed in section 11 ("Good places for Uday to tune") move into one `Laws` struct held by the `Simulation` and handed to the phenotype compiler and the energy rules; a table maps each name to its field. Defaults are today's values, so nothing changes until one is set. A code change under test sits behind a law too (`corpse_reach=1`, `spill_burns=1`), so control and variant run from one binary. The same struct is the "physics fingerprint" creature files are meant to carry (section 5) and what the late-game Laws powers (4.1) will edit: one mechanism for three needs. *Cost:* a few constants become memory reads; check `--ticks 5000` before and after, and leave any that show up in the profile as `constexpr`.
- **As built (2026-11-12, `core/laws.*`):**
  - *One list:* `LUCA_LAWS` in `laws.hpp` holds 115 laws, each with its old constant's name, its default and the file that uses it (energy, phenotype, body, mutation, sim, species). The struct, the name table (`law_table`, `find_law`, case-insensitive) and the parsing all come from that list.
  - *One set per process, not per `Simulation`* (a change from the design above): the phenotype compiler and energy rules are free functions called from the sim, the designer, the test tank, the viewer and the tests, and threading a `Laws` through every call was a large change for no gain, since a fork comparison runs one process per world anyway. `g_laws` is set before a world is built or loaded. Changing it under a running world gives a re-ruled world whose bodies keep their old stats until recompiled.
  - *Use sites unchanged:* each file keeps its constant's name as a reference, `inline const float& METABOLIC_RATE = g_laws.METABOLIC_RATE;`, next to its comment. No rule's code changed.
  - *Exact:* at the defaults, runs are identical to before tick for tick (`--ticks 5000` and 20,000 ticks: every metrics column but `ms_per_tick`, `creatures.csv`, `chronicle.txt`, `species.csv`). One catch: with `SIZE_POWER` a constant 2 the compiler wrote `pow(r, 2)` as `r * r`, which rounds differently from `powf` for about one value in 160, so `contact_flow` now squares directly when the law is 2.
  - *Speed:* 20,000 ticks, three runs each: 0.150 to 0.167 ms/tick before, 0.157 to 0.160 after; no difference beyond noise.
  - *Not done:* switches for code changes under test (`corpse_reach`, `spill_burns`), which come with the changes; laws in creature files (the physics fingerprint); the viewer has no way to set laws.

**3. Forking (`--load world.snap --fork-seed N`).** Loads a snapshot and gives it a new draw seed (and the events' a new one), so one mature world gives any number of different futures. Development-only edits go here too and are never player powers: `--knockout SPECIES` (2.9), `--set`, a dev script line to clear or fill a patch of soil.
- **As built (2026-11-12):** `Simulation::fork(seed)` mixes the fork seed into the sim's draw seed and the events' (`WorldEvents::fork`) and writes a line to the chronicle; the same fork seed always gives the same future (`test_fork_and_knockout`, and headless: two runs of fork 5 give identical metrics). `Simulation::knockout(species)` kills every living member between ticks, as any death: body and store go into the corpse of its cell, the species registry notes each death, and the chronicle says *"Maral is knocked out of the world (17 bodies)."* Both books are unchanged by it (tested). The corpses it leaves are part of the experiment: scavengers get a windfall. Headless: `--fork-seed N` and `--knockout NAME|ID` act only with `--load`. The dev script line for soil is not built.

**4. The comparison (`tools/ab.py`).** Given one or more snapshots, a list of `--set` changes, K fork seeds and T ticks: run control and variant from each snapshot over the same K seeds, one process a core, then report for each metric (population, established species, hunters, movers, scavengers, loose B and C, corpse energy eaten, ms/tick) the mean difference, its spread, and in how many pairs it had the same sign. Twelve pairs of 20,000 ticks cost about what one six-seed sweep from tick 0 costs now. The probes used in this review (meal ledger, death ledger) go behind a `--probe` flag so they are run, not rewritten.
- **As built (2026-11-12):** `tools/ab.py --worlds A.snap B.snap ... --forks K` (or `--seeds 42,7,...` for fresh worlds), `--ticks T`, one or more `--variant "FLAGS"` (several make a sweep, each against the same control), optional `--control`, `--common` (e.g. `"--events 1"`), `--window` (share of each run averaged, default the last fifth), `--probe`, `--jobs`. Control and variant of a pair share the world and the fork seed. It prints, per variant and metric, the control and variant means, the change, its spread between pairs and in how many pairs it went the same way, and the worst audit error; `summary.csv` and every run's own output (`stdout.txt` with the command, metrics, chronicle) stay under `--out` (`ab_out/`, git-ignored). A run that dies out counts as 0. Metrics: population, established species, each way of life, movers, brains, loose B and C, energy in, eaten living and dead, corpses weathered and uptake per tick, ms/tick.
- **The probes** (`TickStats::Probe`, always counted, cheap, read by nothing; headless `--probe` writes `probe.csv` per interval): meals from the living by what became of them (kept, spilled by the `efficiency` share, spilled for want of room, unburnable, and how much was taken by bodies with a store at least 95% full), meals from the dead (arrived and kept), and deaths (all, while being drained, with enough in store but enzymes too slow to release it, with poison as the largest cost, and the store's worth against capacity just before). `ab.py --probe` reports them as shares. Not yet probed: 2.8's causes 2 and 3 (standing gear cost, time between meals), which need per-body state.
- **First use (seed 42 at tick 10,000, 4 forks of 3,000 ticks; a check of the tool, not a result):** `TRANSFER_EFFICIENCY=0.5` halves hunters and scavengers in 3 and 4 pairs of 4, and uptake from the cell rises 69% (the spill comes back through the skin, as 14.2.2 says). Knocking out Maral, the world's one mouthed species (17 bodies), raised mixed feeders in 4 of 4. At tick 10,000, 1.5% of living meals went to bodies with a nearly full store and 0.7% was spilled for want of room, more than the review found at tick 100,000 (0.2 to 0.4%, under 0.1%); the shelf will say whether it falls as the world matures.

**5. A shelf of mature worlds** (`worlds/`, git-ignored, rebuilt by one script): the six usual seeds at tick 100,000, the same with the four test vents, one with events on. Rebuilt whenever a change to the snapshot format cannot be read forward.
- **As built (2026-11-12):** `tools/shelf.py` runs worlds from LUCA and keeps snapshots as `worlds/<kind>_s<seed>_<tick>.snap`; a run's metrics and chronicle go to `worlds/runs/`. A world already on the shelf is continued from its latest snapshot, never restarted, so `--ticks` can be raised later (14.10.1). Kinds: `plain` (30 seeds, 42 / 7 / 99 / 1 / 2 / 3 and 4 to 27, to 100,000 ticks; the 1,000,000-tick extension, keeping 200,000, 500,000 and 1,000,000, is shelved 2026-11-13 and runs with `--ticks 1000000`), `vents` (the usual six with `scripts/four_vents.txt`, cast once at the start, to 100,000) and `events` (the usual six at event rate 1, to 100,000). A world that dies out is kept at its last tick.

**What a fork can and cannot tell you.** A forked world is adapted to the old laws. Its response over 10,000 to 30,000 ticks says what a rule does *to an ecosystem* (who gains, who crashes, does it settle), which is the fast screen. It does not say what evolves under the rule from LUCA. Screen on forks; before adopting, confirm on fresh seeds as now. A rule that helps a fork and hurts a fresh world is worth knowing about either way.
