### 2.8 Rewarding predation: design (proposed 2026-11-12; nothing built)

**Why (Uday, 2026-11-12):** the world is mostly a crowded, uniform mass of sun feeders, and hunters come in booms that die out. These are meant to be pseudo-animals, not microbes: a bear can live a week on one meal, but a hunter here can't go long between meals. The goal is a world that is **stable and diverse**; a population under 3,000 is fine (no cap, ever: the limit comes from the rules).

**Meal size is not the problem.** An eater keeps `TRANSFER_EFFICIENCY` (70%) of a meal, far more than the ~10% real food webs pass up a level. A LUCA-sized kill is worth up to about 625 (70% of a store of up to 650 plus a body of 244), about three days of a LUCA's upkeep (0.35 a tick, 210 a day). What goes wrong is **what a hunter wastes when it eats** and **what it costs to wait between meals**.

**Three causes, from the code:**
1. **A full hunter keeps killing.** `take_in` (phase 2b, `sim.cpp`) keeps what fits in the store and spills the rest, but the drain itself (`contact_flow`, `energy.cpp`) never depends on how much room the taker has. So a hunter with a full store goes on emptying every plant in its mouth's reach, and the energy feeds the plants around it. Prey dies far faster than hunters need it to: the boom-and-bust cycle. *Review 2026-11-12: probed on seeds 42 and 7 (100,000 ticks). Bodies with a store at least 95% full do 0.2% and 0.4% of all draining, and under 0.1% of a meal is spilled for want of room. Stores are almost never full, because breeding empties them. This cause is not supported; see 14.4.*
2. **Gear costs the same whether it is used or not.** Mouth, `bite`, motors, sensors and enzymes all charge upkeep every tick. Enzymes are the worst: under the power cap a mover needs fast enzymes and pays `ENZYME_UPKEEP` × rate around the clock, even while sitting still. A real animal costs little at rest and much when running; a hunter here pays full price all the time, so a LUCA-sized kill lasts it about two days, not a week.
3. **It can't rest on purpose.** Dormancy is only triggered by dark or cold. A full predator can't sleep off a meal like a snake, and a hungry one can't wait cheaply in ambush.

**Ideas, in the order to try them.** Each is one physical rule with a cost; none adds a gene per niche.

- **A. A mouth swallows only what the body has room for (satiation).** Cap the drain at what the taker's store can hold (at `efficiency`, the room it has, for the chemicals it can burn). A full hunter stops killing, and prey survives a partial bite. Ecologists call this a saturating functional response (Holling type II), and it is the textbook fix for predator-prey crashes. Less energy moves, rather than more being spilled, so the books stay simple. **The strongest candidate; build first.** *Review 2026-11-12: not supported by the probe; do not build first (14.4).*
- **B. Split upkeep into resting and working costs.** Motors, mouths and enzymes keep a small standing cost and pay mostly for what they do: enzymes per unit actually turned over, motors per thrust (movement cost already does this), mouths per unit drained. Every gene still has a cost (8.2), but a hunter at rest becomes cheap, and a big hunter with storage could live for days, even a week, on one kill. A real retune: upkeep balances everything today, so judge it on a six-seed sweep.
- **C. Let the brain choose to rest.** A brain output that drives the existing `sleep` level, still capped by the `dormancy` gene. Evolution could wire "full → sleep" (sleeping off a meal) and "hungry, nothing moving → sleep in place" (ambush). The costs are the ones dormancy already has: helpless while asleep, slow to wake.
- **D. Fat cheaper to carry.** Storage already has low upkeep (0.02 per unit of size), but its mass (0.4) feeds the Kleiber term and movement cost. Counting storage mass at, say, half in the Kleiber term would make "eat big, fast long" a viable body plan. A smaller lever: try it only if A to C fall short.
- **E. Kills worth coming back to (no rule of its own).** Corpses weather slowly (0.4% every 8 ticks), so a kill is already a meal that lasts. With A, a sated hunter leaves a corpse behind, and returning to it is something slow CTRNN neurons could learn.

**On the crowd of sun feeders:** no fix for plants yet. Stable predators are one of the best-known sources of diversity: a predator that keeps the dominant competitor in check makes room for others (Paine's keystone predators). The uniformity may be the absence of steady predation. Spatial refuges help predators and prey coexist; water (2.7, stages 1 to 3) is the planned source of those.

**Order of work:**
1. **Probe first** (six seeds, 100,000 ticks): what share of what hunters drain is spilled because their store is full (cause 1); what share of a hunter's upkeep is standing gear cost (cause 2); how long hunters live between meals. *Cause 1 was probed on two seeds 2026-11-12 and came back negative (14.4). Probes for causes 2 and 3 built 2026-11-12, observation only, in `probe.csv` (headless `--probe`): `mouth_gear_share` and `mover_gear_share` (the share of upkeep paid by bodies with a mouth, or able to move, that is standing gear: mouths, motors, senses, bite, brain and enzymes, `Phenotype::gear_upkeep`), `mean_meal_gap` (ticks between meals from the living, for bodies that had eaten before) and `mean_fast_at_death` (ticks since such a body's last meal when it died). One continuation of seed 42 (6,000 ticks on from 30,000): gear is 17% of a mouth's upkeep and 32% of a mover's; meals come 29 ticks apart; a body that had hunted dies 220 ticks after its last meal. Six seeds still to run.*
2. **Build A**, with the usual checks (no warnings, `--ticks 5000` against 10.7, `test_powers`, `test_life`) and a six-seed run. *Judge:* hunters persist without a crash in every seed; established species rise; prey survives bites.
3. Add **B** or **C** only if A is not enough, one at a time, each judged on six seeds. D last.

*Step 3 run 2026-11-13. Six shelf worlds (seeds 42 / 7 / 99 / 1 / 2 / 3, 6,000 ticks on from 100,000, `--probe`): cause 1 negative again (full stores do 0.13 to 0.29% of the draining); standing gear is 16 to 19% of a mouth-bearer's upkeep and 19 to 30% of a mover's; 70 to 84% of deaths are drained (eaten), not starved; a body that had hunted dies 80 to 120 ticks after its last meal; meals come 150 to 530 ticks apart. Idea B screened as an upper bound with the law `GEAR_STANDING` (default 1; below 1 gear is cheaper to keep and nothing charges for using it), 30 shelf worlds, one fork each, 20,000 ticks: at 0.5 hunters +59%, movers +81%, living eaten +34%, but sun-fed -16%, population -2%, species -1%; at 0.2 hunters +81%, movers +73%, sun-fed -31%, population -12%, species -10%. Hunters and movers rise; stability and diversity (the judge) do not. Not adopted; the law stays at 1. Per-use charging would only lower the gain. 14 of 90 forks (controls included) were already dead on the shelf and count as zeros. Next: C belongs to step 5 (the brain).*

**Open before building:** whether a sated mouth stops pulling outright or pulls at a rate scaled by room left; whether B's resting share is one constant for all parts or one per part; how C's rest output interacts with the dark and cold trigger (the deeper of the two, or their sum).
