### 2.7 Water layer: design (proposed 2026-11-12; nothing built)

**Why:** the world is too uniform (section 11, "What the game is lacking", point 3), and the chemistry layer is stuck: loose B and C stay at a few units a world and sit in the cell they fell in, so nobody can live on another's leavings (milestone 5). Water is one physical layer that changes how existing rules act. It adds **no gene per niche** (see memory: general rules over special genes): what a body can do in water comes from the parts and enzymes it already has.

**Rules for every stage:**
- Water holds no energy, so the energy book is untouched. Matter moves only through rules that keep `total_matter()` exact, except the sediment sink (stage 4), which is booked like vents.
- Every water field is a static or slow field (recomputed when terrain or sea level changes, or every 8 ticks), never per creature per tick (7.3 rule 7).
- Powers that change water go through `World`'s edit functions and the power log, like terrain.
- Build **one stage at a time**. Each stage ends with the usual checks (no warnings, `--ticks 5000` against 10.7, `test_powers`, `test_life`), a six-seed 100,000-tick run (42 / 7 / 99 / 1 / 2 / 3), and Uday's go-ahead before the next stage.

**Stages, in order:**

1. **Sea level and light under water.** A world `sea_level` (config, default a level that drowns roughly a third of the default map's lowest ground); `depth` = max(0, sea − height) per cell, a static field like `World::shade()`. Light falls off with depth, `exp(−depth / LIGHT_DEPTH)` (Beer–Lambert), on top of slope shade. A global `sea` power raises or lowers the sea (instant or as a ramp, logged and replayable). Viewer: water tiles in depth bands with the terrain's ordered dither, a slow shimmer, and a "depth" overlay. Nothing else changes yet: bodies treat water as ground. *Judge:* the world still lives on six seeds; photo patch size and canopy height zone by depth, without code telling them to.
2. **Water as a solvent.** A cell's wetness (1 under water, 0 on dry land; later the moisture field) scales the chemistry rules: loose chemicals spread faster (`WASTE_SPREAD` × (1 + `WET_SPREAD` × wetness), split over more steps if it would pass 1) and skin uptake is faster (`UPTAKE_RATE` × (1 + `WET_UPTAKE` × wetness)). On land, leavings stay put; in the sea they mix. *Judge:* loose B and C rise in the sea; look for cross-feeding (milestone 5) and diet splits by medium. This is the stage aimed at the chemistry layer's open problem. *Review 2026-11-12: mixing cannot raise loose B and C while no body gives them off; see 14.2.7 and 14.4.*
3. **A medium for movement.** Each motor part's thrust and cost depend on the medium: flagella work well in water and poorly on land, legs the other way round (numbers to pick). Buoyancy: in water, climbing costs nothing and the per-mass part of moving cost is cut (`BUOYANCY`), so a large body is cheaper to carry. A step from water onto land or back is allowed; the motor rule alone decides who can make it. Chronicle detectors: a species living mostly on land after its ancestors lived mostly in water ("crawls ashore"), and the reverse. *Judge:* swimmers and walkers split by medium; bodies are bigger in water.
4. **Sinking and the sediment sink.** Under water, corpses and loose chemicals drift down-slope (along `World::downhill()`, a share every 8 ticks), so the deep lives on what falls from above. In cells deeper than `SEDIMENT_DEPTH`, a small share of soil A settles out of the cycle each pass, booked as `matter_out` (`total_matter() - matter_brought_up() + matter_buried()` stays constant). This is the sink the vents need (Known weak spots: matter piles up around vents); it is Uday's call whether vents should sit under water by default. *Judge:* scavengers and cleaners in the deep; matter around vents bounded.
5. **Currents.** A fixed drift field over the sea (from the coastline's shape, or along the long axis of each basin; a choice to make when built) carries loose chemicals, corpses and newborns a share of a cell each pass. Children landing in water drift for free, so a plant in the sea spreads without `seed_throw`; on land, dispersal still costs. A `current` brush may follow. *Judge:* sea plants carry less `seed_throw` than land plants; plankton-like drifters.
6. **Heat buffer and ice.** Water's temperature follows the air far more slowly than any body (a `WATER_LAG` like `HEAT_LAG`), so the sea has no cold nights and gentle climate swings. Where the water surface falls below freezing it ices over: light under ice is cut to a share, and the water below stays at about 4 °C. *Judge:* life persists under the ice through a snowball Earth (scenario 4.4); land bodies are hardier and sleep more than sea bodies.
7. **Rivers.** Flow accumulation along `World::downhill()` (how many cells drain through each) gives river channels as a static field, recomputed after a terrain stroke like slope shade. River cells count as wet (stage 2) and carry loose chemicals and washed soil downstream to the sea, faster than plain washing. Barriers for walkers that are not swimmers. *Judge:* life follows rivers inland; rivers split populations.
8. **Tides.** The sea level swings by `TIDE` with the day (or on a period of its own), so a band of shore is dry for part of every day. Uses the stage 1 field with a moving level; the depth field must then be cheap to recompute or computed as height against a level. *Judge:* an intertidal niche: dormancy and armour more common in the tidal band.
9. **Drying out (optional, last).** Water becomes a chemical, W, held in the store (2.1.1). On land a body loses W through its skin each tick, faster in sun and heat; in water and on wet ground it refills by uptake. Armour slows the loss over the arc it covers (a cuticle: a second use for an existing part, no new gene). Possibly the uphill enzymes need W as an input (light running A + W → D + …), making dry ridges sunny but thirsty and valleys dim but watered. A body out of W dies like one out of energy. Built only if land life turns out too easy after stages 1 to 8; it touches the chemistry books and the designer. *Judge:* colonising land is rare and late, and a visible event when it happens.

**Moisture field (with stage 7 or 9):** land wetness from distance to water and rivers, plus rain if wanted, gives deserts and wetlands. Not a stage of its own; built when the first stage that needs it is.

**Open before building:** the default sea level and `LIGHT_DEPTH`; whether creatures see water (a brain input for depth or wetness, needed for anything to *seek* the shore); whether the parade and designer test tank get a pond; how far each stage moves the six-seed baseline before the next is started.
